Live well and use medicines safely

Practical advice from your Family Doctor · Page 2/2

1. Safe use of medicines and prevention

Medicines every day

High blood pressure, diabetes or cholesterol: take your medicines every day even if you feel well. Never stop them on your own.

Antibiotics

Take them only if the doctor prescribes them (they do not work for viruses, colds or flu). Always finish the whole box.

Herbs and traditional remedies

Always tell your doctor if you use herbal teas, herbs, supplements or traditional medicines: they can change the effect of your medicines.

Free vaccines and screening

They protect you and your family from serious diseases. Ask the doctor which vaccines and free check-ups are right for you.

2. Food and daily movement

A healthy plate every day

Eat more green leafy vegetables, pulses (lentils, chickpeas) and wholegrains. Cut down on white rice, white bread, fried food and sweets.

Less salt, more spices

Too much salt raises blood pressure. Use spices, garlic, lemon and natural herbs to flavour your food.

Drink plain water

Drink 1.5–2 litres of water a day. Avoid fizzy drinks, sweetened tea and packaged fruit juices (they contain a lot of sugar).

Move for 30 minutes a day

Walk briskly every day: it lowers blood sugar, regulates blood pressure, controls weight and reduces stress.

3. Mind wellbeing and rest

Your mind needs care too

Sadness, anxiety or deep tiredness are not your fault. If you can no longer do your usual things, talk to your doctor without fear.

Sleep 7–8 hours and keep a routine

Sleep repairs mind and body. Keep regular hours, turn off phone screens before bed and get some fresh air every day.
